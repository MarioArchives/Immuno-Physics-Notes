How a well-adapted immune system is organized

Andreas Mayer, Vijay Balasubramanian, Thierry Mora and Aleksandra M. Walczak. PNAS 112, 5950–5955 (2015). Published version, also free on PubMed Central; arXiv preprint.

What receptor repertoire minimizes the expected cost of infections from a known mix of pathogens, why cross-reactivity breaks it into a sparse set of clones, and how competition for antigens can reach it.

How to read these notes

Each page follows one part of the paper, with the derivations from its appendix folded in where they belong, and explains its arguments and equations in plain terms; the wording is new and the paper remains the reference. The paper's sections are not numbered, so the notes number them 1 to 6.

The page links in each part open the arXiv preprint of July 2014, which is free to read and has the same sections. The notes follow the version published in PNAS in 2015, which adds a few robustness checks (other cross-reactivity kernels, receptor-specific widths, avoidance of self) and a stochastic version of the competitive dynamics, and whose supplement letters the appendices differently.

Underlined abbreviations open a short definition when clicked, and the glossary lists all of them. The framed figures are small simulations of the models in the text, several of them solving the optimization live: they start when they scroll into view, and their controls change the parameters under discussion.
